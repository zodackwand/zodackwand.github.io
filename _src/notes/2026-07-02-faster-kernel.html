title: Two weeks on a kernel, beaten by a library description: I wrote a fused kernel. The vendor library was faster. Here is what the two weeks actually bought. ---

The profile said sixty-one per cent of the step was in one fused block. The arithmetic said that block was moving four times more memory than it needed to. Those two facts together make the most seductive sentence in systems work, and I spent two weeks following it.

The napkin version, before any code:

tokens      = 8192
hidden      = 4096
bytes/elem  = 2            (bf16)
round trips = 4            (what the naive version does)

traffic = 8192 * 4096 * 2 * 4  =  268 MB
at ~2.6 TB/s                   =  103 us

measured                       =  390 us

A factor of about four. That is exactly the size of gap that makes you believe you are one clever rewrite away from closing it, and exactly the size of gap that is usually made of six unglamorous things rather than one clever one.

What happened

I got to 190 µs. Then I benchmarked the vendor library, which I had skipped because I assumed it did not fuse this pattern. It does, as of a version whose release notes I had not read. It runs in 171 µs.

So: two weeks of work produced a kernel ten per cent slower than one line of someone else's code.

What the two weeks bought

Not nothing, and I want to be careful about why, because "it was a learning experience" is what people say instead of "I was wrong".

  • I know the shape of the roofline for this operator now, so next time a profile points here I can decide in ten minutes rather than ten days.
  • Half the speedup came from a layout change upstream of the kernel. That change survived, and it helps the library version too.
  • Our benchmark harness was measuring with the cache warm in a way production never is. That was worth the fortnight on its own.

The lesson is duller than the one I wanted: benchmark the boring option first. Not because it usually wins, but because knowing where it lands is what tells you whether the interesting option is worth two weeks.
