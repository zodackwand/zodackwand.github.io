Two weeks on a kernel, beaten by a library

2 July 2026
Home Notes Research

The profile said sixty-one per cent of the step was in one fused block. The arithmetic said that block was moving four times more memory than it needed to. Those two facts together are the most seductive sentence in systems work, and I spent two weeks following it.

The napkin version, before any code:

tokens      = 8192
hidden      = 4096
bytes/elem  = 2            (bf16)
round trips = 4            (what the naive version does)

traffic = 8192 * 4096 * 2 * 4  =  268 MB
at ~2.6 TB/s                   =  103 us

measured                       =  390 us

A factor of about four. That is exactly the size of gap that makes you believe you are one clever rewrite away from it, and exactly the size of gap that is usually made of six unglamorous things rather than one clever one.

What actually happened

I got to 190 µs. Then I ran the vendor library, which I had not benchmarked because I had assumed it did not fuse this pattern. It does, as of some version I had not read the notes for. It runs in 171 µs.

The honest summary is that two weeks of work produced a kernel that is ten per cent slower than one line of someone else's code.

What the two weeks bought

I do not think they were wasted, and I want to be careful about why, because "it was a learning experience" is the sort of thing people say to avoid saying "I was wrong".

  • I now know the shape of the roofline for this operator, so the next time a profile points here I can decide in ten minutes rather than ten days.
  • Half of the speedup came from a layout change upstream of the kernel. That change survived; it helps the library version too.
  • I found that our benchmark harness was measuring with the cache warm in a way production never is. That was worth the fortnight on its own.

The lesson I am actually taking is smaller and duller than the one I wanted: benchmark the boring option first. Not because it usually wins — it does not always — but because knowing where it lands is what tells you whether the interesting option is worth two weeks.
