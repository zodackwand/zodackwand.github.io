The index that forgot where the questions came from

14 Aug 2026

A graph index is built once, over the vectors you happen to have. Which neighbours a node keeps, and which long edges earn their memory, are decisions taken against that data. They amount to a bet that later questions will look like it.

When the bet fails, it fails quietly. Recall does not collapse, it sags. Ninety-six per cent becomes eighty-nine, the latency budget absorbs a few extra hops, and the dashboard shows a line slightly worse than last month. Nobody files a bug for slightly worse.

A small experiment

Take a million embeddings, build the usual graph, then ask questions embedded by a different model of the same family. The vectors still live in the same space and are still normalised. In aggregate they simply sit slightly to one side.

Query sourceRecall@10Hops
Same encoder0.96241
Sibling encoder0.89163
Sibling, re-entered0.94844

The third row is what I found interesting. Nothing about the graph changed. The search simply starts somewhere else: at the medoid of a few hundred cached queries from the last hour, rather than at the entry point chosen at build time. Most of the loss was never a property of the graph. It was a property of where the walk began.

The cost of the two fixes

Re-entering costs a few hundred vectors of memory and one median update an hour. Rebuilding the index costs an afternoon of compute and a deploy. We tend to reach for the rebuild because it is the intervention we have a runbook for, which is a poor reason.

Re-entry is not a general answer. It corrects the part of the mismatch that looks like a translation and does nothing for the part that looks like a change of shape. But a graph index has two inputs, and we usually measure only one of them.
