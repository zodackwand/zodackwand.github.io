I work on making retrieval and generation cheap enough to be boring. Mostly that means measuring things carefully, discovering the bottleneck is somewhere I did not expect, and then rewriting the part I was proudest of.

This page is hand-written HTML and one stylesheet. It loads in under a kilobyte of CSS, has no tracker, no font to download, and no JavaScript. If it ever needs a build step, something has gone wrong.

Currently

Nearest-neighbour search when the queries move. Graph indices are built over the data you have. The queries arrive later, from somewhere else, and nobody tells the index. I am trying to say precisely what that costs and what you can cheaply do about it.

Few-step generative models. Learning average velocity fields rather than instantaneous ones, so that sampling is one or two forward passes instead of fifty. The interesting part is where the approximation breaks.

Napkin math as a discipline. A running collection of the arithmetic I wish someone had shown me before I started profiling: bytes moved, cache lines touched, the ratio between what the hardware could do and what we ask of it.
